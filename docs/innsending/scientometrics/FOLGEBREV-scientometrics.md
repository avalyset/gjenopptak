To the Editor-in-Chief, *Scientometrics*

**Submission: "Declared and undone: an open tool for finding work that researchers report leaving unfinished, and a test of whether the named obstacles can be lifted" (Original Paper)**

Dear Editor,

I submit the manuscript above for consideration as an Original Paper.

**What the study is.** Researchers routinely write down work they did not do and name the obstacle. The study measures how such declared-undone passages can be found in published text, what obstacles they name, and whether those obstacles can be lifted. From 100 open-access works (2015–2020) in four fields and 22 243 passages, an open pipeline produced a candidate list of 432 rows; among them, roughly two thirds name data that never existed. A second, prospective run on 100 new archaeology works met a precision gate fixed before the draw (85 of 100) and gave the first measured recall of the sieve (95 of 104 reference hits retained). Six reopening attempts lifted none of the named obstacles: what limited them was access and consent, not model capability.

**Why *Scientometrics*.** The paper is a quantitative study of research reporting — of what authors record as left undone and why — with the measurement properties of language-model coders reported as part of the contribution. That places it in the science of science that the journal covers.

**Preregistration.** The protocol (PREREG-v1) was locked internally on 12 September 2026, before data were read, and corrected only through dated addenda.
